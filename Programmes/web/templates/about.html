About DbCAS

DbCAS was created to address a growing challenge in the RNA splicing field: the rapid expansion and fragmentation of alternative splicing databases across platforms, organisms, technologies, and analytical frameworks. As long-read sequencing, single-cell profiling, and context-specific transcriptomics proliferate, researchers increasingly struggle to locate, compare, and contextualise the resources most relevant to their work. DbCAS brings these resources together in a single, coherent commons — making navigation and discovery easier and the landscape more transparent for the splicing community.

DbCAS was built through a systematic literature-mining workflow that began with 16,212 splicing-related publications retrieved across 2014–2026. Backward citation expansion and identity-based deduplication grew the candidate pool to 383,439 publications, from which keyword filtering, two model-assisted screening steps, and evidence-based website verification produced a curated collection of 287 databases covering 109 species and taxa. Resource accessibility is monitored continuously, and the collection is updated regularly.

This initiative is led by Dr Kif Liakath-Ali, Principal Investigator of SpliceLab, University of Southampton, with the goal of supporting open, accessible, and integrative RNA splicing research worldwide.
